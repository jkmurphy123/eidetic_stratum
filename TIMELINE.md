# The Eidetic Stratum: A Historical Timeline

*Working canon for the Alderwick newspaper setting, through approximately 1880.*

## Historical frame

The ordinary historical record holds until the late seventeenth century. The Stratum has always existed, but its investigation begins as an obscure branch of optics and natural philosophy. The dates attached to Newton, Maraldi, Fresnel, Poisson, and Arago follow real optical history; their apparent involvement with the Eidetic Stratum is fiction. Other named researchers, societies, incidents, instruments, and discoveries below are invented for this world.

The word **portal** has two historical meanings. The **Maraldi aperture** of 1723 was a momentary opening that no one recognized as such. The **Arago aperture** of 1818 was the first deliberately observed, reproducible opening. A stable opening large enough for a probe followed decades later. Thus 1723 is remembered as the first opening, while 1818 is the birth of portal science.

## Precursors: 1660–1722

**1666–1672 — Newton's spectral anomaly.** In the fictional account, Isaac Newton notices that a minute fraction of light at the edges of certain prism shadows appears displaced without a corresponding change in color. He records the effect as an imperfection of glass and declines to claim another realm. His real optical investigations establish the experimental language later researchers adopt.

**1694 — The second-shadow notebooks.** Natural philosophers in Leiden and London compare older accounts of doubles, ideal forms, and Plato's cave with measurements of optical shadows. Most dismiss the effort as occult speculation; a few begin the useful practice of recording instrument geometry alongside testimony. No one yet has evidence of a traversable place.

**1704 — A new reading of optics.** Newton's *Opticks* gives natural philosophers a common set of experiments. In this world's private correspondence, the unexplained shadow displacement becomes known as the *remainder*: the part of an observation left after ordinary light is accounted for.

**1711 — The geological analogy.** Surveyor and instrument maker Miriam Vale compares the optical remainder to a concealed geological bed: measurable by its effect without being seen directly. Her manuscript uses *stratum* provisionally, prompting investigations of mineral composition and optical response.

## The aperture and its century of uncertainty: 1723–1817

**1723 — Maraldi's bright point; the first accidental portal.** Giacomo Maraldi observes a bright point at the center of a circular object's shadow. In the fiction, one exposure also leaves a fine ring of altered silver on his receiving plate. The anomalous mark is filed away. Later Eideticians classify the fleeting central point as the first recorded Eidetic aperture, although no contemporary observer knows what opened.

**1738 — Breakthrough 1: The paired-shadow assay.** Vale's successors show that two carefully matched plates occasionally develop shadows with the same central brightness but different fine edge structures. They identify a repeatable *residual* beyond ordinary illumination; this remains an anomaly, not proof of a second reality.

**1756 — Breakthrough 2: The referent hypothesis.** Éloise Varenne proposes that every enduring object has a deeper *Referent*, and that material objects are imperfect expressions of it. She derives a testable prediction: changing an object's substance while holding its form constant should alter the residual less than changing its form. This is the first useful Eidetic theory.

**1764 — The discipline receives its name.** In the spirit of geological surveys and Enlightenment classification, the Alderwick Society of Natural Inquiry adopts **Eidetic Stratum** for the proposed underlying order, **Eidetics** for its study, and **Eidetician** for an investigator. The terminology spreads before the hypothesis is generally accepted.

**1771 — Breakthrough 3: Form survives material substitution.** Repeated assays of identical shapes made from different metals yield related residual patterns. Opponents point to contamination and instrument bias; supporters argue that geometry, rather than substance alone, couples ordinary matter to its Referent.

**1792 — Breakthrough 4: The concordance scale.** Astrid Bell devises a numerical measure of how closely an object's observed residual matches a standard form. *Concordance* makes independent laboratories' findings comparable. Two ostensibly identical objects can now be said to have measurably different relationships with their Referents.

**1806 — Breakthrough 5: The interference lull.** Researchers find that the residual becomes easier to detect when certain wave patterns cancel ordinary illumination. The result directs investigators toward central shadow spots and helps explain why earlier observers confused the effect with diffraction.

## Recognition and experimental entry: 1818–1851

**1818 — Fresnel, Poisson, Arago, and the recognized aperture.** In real optical history, Poisson drew a surprising bright-center prediction from Fresnel's wave theory, and Arago verified the spot experimentally. In this fictional history, Arago's apparatus also produces a second, instrument-dependent response within the bright center. Subsequent controlled trials distinguish the real diffraction spot from a tiny, conditional Eidetic opening. Public accounts celebrate the vindication of wave optics; a smaller circle begins a separate research program on the opening.

**1822 — Breakthrough 6: Aperture threshold.** Léonie Marchand shows that a bright spot by itself is insufficient: a particular combination of alignment, surface finish, and concordance produces an opening. This prevents Eideticians from calling every Poisson spot a portal.

**1829 — Breakthrough 7: Reciprocal impression.** A prepared silver film placed against an opening acquires marks even when ordinary light and particles are screened away. A shaped interruption on the laboratory side alters the pattern returned. Investigators establish that something can be sent *toward* the Stratum and something can answer.

**1837 — Breakthrough 8: Eidetic sonance.** Small changes in the mismatch between an object and its Referent propagate as patterned fluctuations through nearby apertures. These are called *sonances*: reality-borne emissions that can be received, reflected, and eventually broadcast. They are neither airborne sound nor radio waves.

**1844 — Breakthrough 9: The prime referent and first probe.** The first stable aperture admits a tethered brass probe. Its departure point is designated the **Prime Referent**, origin of the survey coordinates. The probe returns bearing a pale mineral deposit; **retrieval** becomes the general term for material recovered from the Stratum. Its path appears to cross a succession of layers rather than a continuous landscape.

**1851 — Breakthrough 10: Layer reckoning.** Surveys show that distance within the Stratum cannot be expressed by ordinary latitude and longitude. Eideticians record a destination by its layer, its relation to the Prime Referent, and its measured sonance offset. The practice becomes the basis of Stratum navigation, although routes can drift after major aperture events.

## The age of surveys: 1852–1880

**1858 — Breakthrough 11: The return discrepancy.** Matching clocks carried through an aperture return with small but consistent differences from clocks kept outside. Time in a layer is locally orderly, but the relation between layers is not fixed. Expeditions begin carrying duplicate chronometers and reporting both dates.

**1863 — Breakthrough 12: Referential fatigue.** Long exposure to strong sonance causes familiar objects to deviate subtly from their previous shapes or functions. The effect is most pronounced for objects whose concordance has already weakened. The Society limits unattended portal operation and requires sealed storage for retrievals.

**1868 — The Alderwick receiving hall opens.** The city funds a public observatory, receiving room, and controlled expedition chamber. Daily sonance bulletins turn obscure laboratory readings into matters of commerce, travel, and public anxiety; newspaper correspondents gain permanent desks beside the surveyors.

**1870 — Breakthrough 13: The echo relay.** A tuned Referent can carry a patterned sonance to a distant receiver without an open portal at the receiving end. Messages are short and prone to ambiguities when nearby objects share a form. Relay offices, private codes, and disputes over intercepted transmissions follow.

**1874 — Breakthrough 14: The custody test.** A retrieval can acquire characteristics associated with the name, records, and handling history assigned to it after recovery. Researchers disagree whether observers change its Referent or merely reveal a preexisting one. Museums record an object's chain of custody as carefully as its composition.

**1877 — Breakthrough 15: Layer weather.** Coordinated stations detect periodic changes in sonance, aperture stability, and route position across several layers. A forecast does not make a crossing safe, but it identifies dangerous intervals. Shipping insurers, scientific societies, and newspaper editors all seek advance reports.

**1880 — The unsettled present.** The public knows that apertures, sonances, retrievals, and layer surveys exist; it does not know whether the Stratum precedes the material world, reflects it, or changes with human observation. Alderwick is a center of journals, expeditions, patent claims, missing-person notices, and curious classified advertisements. This is the natural present tense for the newspaper.

## Canon anchors and open questions

- **Prime Referent:** the first stable probe's point of departure in 1844; coordinate zero, not necessarily the center of the Stratum.
- **Referent:** the deeper counterpart associated with an ordinary object; its exact nature remains disputed.
- **Sonance:** measurable or transmissible variation in the relationship between an object and its Referent.
- **Retrieval:** any object or substance brought back from the Stratum; provenance and custody matter.
- **Aperture:** an opening produced under controlled optical and referential conditions; an ordinary diffraction spot alone is insufficient.
- **Open disputes:** whether a Referent can exist without a material counterpart; whether layers are discovered or created by traversal; who may own a retrieval; and whether the 1723 opening left anything in our world.

## Real-history reference points

The fiction intentionally adds Stratum interpretations to real optical episodes. For real-world grounding, see [the Newton Project's account of Newton's optical work](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/tour/newtons-optical-work), [Newton's *Opticks* (1704)](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00035), and [Kelly et al., “First- and second-order Poisson spots” (*American Journal of Physics*, 2009)](https://pubs.aip.org/aapt/ajp/article/77/8/713/310887/First-and-second-order-Poisson-spots). The 1723 Maraldi attribution and the 1818 Fresnel–Poisson–Arago episode are discussed in historical accounts of the spot; no historical source asserts an Eidetic aperture.
